IELTS band scores explained

IELTS results are reported on a scale from 0 to 9, in whole and half bands, for each section and overall. There is no pass or fail: each organisation sets the score it needs.

The nine bands

The official site names the skill level of each band. In brief, in our words:

BandOfficial labelIn short
9ExpertFull command: accurate, fluent and appropriate, with complete understanding.
8Very goodFull command with only occasional slips; handles complex argument well.
7GoodGood command with some inaccuracies and misunderstandings; generally handles complex language.
6CompetentEffective command despite errors; copes with fairly complex language in familiar situations.
5ModestPartial command; gets the overall meaning in most situations but makes many mistakes.
4LimitedBasic competence in familiar situations only; frequent problems.
3Extremely limitedOnly general meaning in very familiar situations.
2IntermittentGreat difficulty with spoken and written English.
1Non-userA few isolated words at most.
0Did not attempt the testNo questions answered.

How the overall band is worked out

The overall band is the average of the four section bands, rounded to the nearest half band. The official rule for the awkward cases: an average ending in .25 is rounded up to the next half band, and one ending in .75 is rounded up to the next whole band.

The official site gives these examples. Section scores of 6.5, 6.5, 5.0 and 7.0 average 6.25, so the overall band is 6.5. Scores of 4.0, 3.5, 4.0 and 4.0 average 3.875, giving 4.0. Scores of 6.5, 6.5, 5.5 and 6.0 average 6.125, giving 6.0. Try your own with the band calculator.

Listening and Reading: marks to bands

Each has 40 questions, one mark each, and the mark out of 40 is converted to a band. The official site gives the average marks at four levels and warns that the exact number varies slightly from one test version to another.

Notice that General Training Reading needs more correct answers for the same band. The official explanation is that Academic texts may be harder in vocabulary and style, and both are reported on the same scale. Full conversion tables found on other websites are estimates; only these points are published by IELTS on the page we read.

Writing and Speaking

These are marked by examiners against four criteria each, equally weighted. For Writing: task achievement or task response, coherence and cohesion, lexical resource, and grammatical range and accuracy. For Speaking: fluency and coherence, lexical resource, grammatical range and accuracy, and pronunciation. Each is explained on its own page in Writing and Speaking.

How long is a score valid?

The official site recommends that results be considered valid for two years after the test, and notes that organisations may choose to accept them for longer. Check with the organisation you are applying to.
